# Making BLKFNDR usable without Web3 knowledge

> **Redesign brief for Claude Design.** Prepared 2026-10-02 from `main` (a4ffb48) and the live testnet site. Six researchers walked every user flow as a persona with a Gmail account, a debit card and a phone and no crypto background (163 friction points, 15 of them hard blockers). Three designers proposed competing approaches, three judges scored them, and the hybrid below was synthesized, then audited in three rounds for anything custodial or semi-custodial.
>
> **Hard rule (owner, 2026-10-02): non-custodial, no exceptions.** Nothing in this brief gives BLKFNDR a key, a recovery signer, a co-signer, or a chokepoint over a stakeholder's or builder's money or vote. The allowed toolbox is: passkey smart wallets whose only signer is the user's device, a multi-wallet connect modal, fee sponsorship that only pays the network fee and always has a self-funded fallback, a permissionless executor that can delay but never decide, and third-party on-ramps that pay the user's own address.
>
> **Seen on the live site (2026-10-02, phone viewport, signed out):** project cards and the project dialog print full 56-character Stellar addresses beside builder names; the primary button reads "Fund with USDC" and the goal line says "raised"; pressing that button while signed out produced no response at all, not even a sign-in prompt.
>
> **How to use it:** paste the Design principles and the Vocabulary table into Claude Design as standing instructions, then paste one "Prompt N" block at a time, in order. The last section has the full steps.

## Summary

**Diagnosis.** A person with Gmail, a debit card and a phone can read everything on BLKFNDR but cannot do anything with money. Every stake, vote, refund and launch ends at the same red toast asking them to install the Freighter browser extension, which does not exist on a phone. Before that wall they are never told a wallet is coming, amounts show as USDC and XLM instead of dollars, "testnet" is never explained, a phantom 3% fee is shown but never charged by the contract, raw contract errors reach the toast, and five different words describe the one act of staking.

**Direction.** The three design studies and three judges converge on one hybrid. Phase 1 is the honesty pass all three share: practice-mode banner, dollars first, plain status words, one vocabulary, a sign-in that returns to the project, a stake form readable before any gate, and an error voice that says whether money moved. Phase 2 takes the Guided self-custody architecture (Stellar Wallets Kit so phones get Lobstr and xBull, a preview-before-popup Confirm overlay, a Funds check sheet, fee-bump sponsorship from the existing gas-only ops key) plus the Tiered approach's governance cron, in-product Record tab and Decisions inbox. Phase 3 adds the Invisible Chain idea, a passkey wallet created on the first stake so a phone user approves with Face ID, gated behind three hard conditions from the trust review. Real-money on-ramps and builders without a wallet come last.

**What ships first.** Copy, information architecture and flow changes that touch no chain code, in about two weeks: the persona will understand the product, compare projects in dollars, come back to the project after Google sign-in, read what staking involves, verify identity without a wallet, and never again see a hash, a contract code or a vendor name. Every Phase 1 prompt carries a labelled Phase 1 variant wherever a later phase changes the words, and every Phase 1 variant is honest about what is not yet possible (no practice dollars, no phone wallet) instead of pointing at a tab that cannot help.

## Design principles

1. **Nobody needs a wallet to read.** Every vault, vote, proof and payout is readable with no account, and the money record is a tab in the product. Example: "$1,000 paid to the builder for Stage 1" with a "Verify this" panel per row.
2. **Dollars first, tokens on request.** Amounts render as $; USDC and XLM codes, addresses, hashes and network fees sit under a "Technical details" disclosure. Example: a card reads "$3,200 of $5,000 staked · 12 days left".
3. **Say what the next step needs before the person invests effort.** Example: the sign-in sheet says "To move money you'll also set up a wallet you control; we'll guide you right after this".
4. **One idea per gate, and the form is readable before the gate.** Example: "Stake from $5" always opens the stake sheet; sign-in, wallet and funds are cards inside it.
5. **Every popup is previewed on our page before it opens.** Example: an annotated mock of the wallet window with "Press Approve here" and a "Can't see the window?" link.
6. **Every failure says what happened, whether money moved, and the one button that fixes it.** Example: "Not confirmed. Nothing was moved or charged. Try again."
7. **The platform pays the plumbing but never holds the money, and says where the line is.** Example: "Network fee: covered by BLKFNDR. Covering a fee cannot change, add to or replay what you approved."
8. **One word per idea, everywhere, and never a promise a phase has not made true.** Example: stake, stakeholder, vault, stage, payout, builder's deposit, practice mode; "Pay by card" appears only once a provider is live.

## The recommended experience

### Discovery

What changes: a practice-mode banner above the header; desktop text nav (Projects · How it works · Open a vault) and a visible "Sign in" button; hero rewritten around the vault and the vote; a "Three ways to take part" row (Watch, Stake, Steward) that discloses what each level needs and where practice dollars come from; the eight feature cards folded into six plain security cards; cards with a money row, days left, stakeholder count, plain status pills with tooltips and no public key; filters in dollars; sha256, build command, test names and contract IDs collapsed under "For auditors and developers".
Custody stays honest: the hero says "Nobody at BLKFNDR can open a vault" and the footer says "Stakes are held in each project's vault, never in a BLKFNDR account." The auditor's drawer keeps every hash so the claim stays checkable.
Vocabulary: vault, stake, stakeholder, builder's deposit, stage, "Open for stakes", "Goal reached", "Building", "Returning money", "Awaiting builder's deposit", "Paused by BLKFNDR", practice mode.

### Join and first wallet

What changes: one sign-in sheet that stays on the page, carries the project and intent through Google OAuth and email confirmation, and tells the truth about the second step. Phase 1 wraps the existing Freighter connect in a wizard shell whose phone state says plainly that phone wallets are not supported yet. Phase 2 fills the wizard with Stellar Wallets Kit (Freighter, xBull, Albedo on desktop; Lobstr, xBull on phones once the device spike passes), explains what a wallet is, insists on the recovery-words checkbox, detects the network, previews both popups, and ends with "Your wallet is linked to your account." The link is durable: sign-out never unlinks; "Disconnect for now" and "Remove wallet from account" are separate actions. Phase 3 adds a passkey wallet created on the first stake, so a phone user approves with Face ID.
Custody stays honest: the wizard's first line is "A wallet holds the key to your money. It lives on your device, not with BLKFNDR." The passkey tier ships only when the wallet wasm is audited and hash-pinned, the client verifies on-chain that the deployed wallet runs the pinned, audited wasm and that its sole signer is the just-created passkey, and the Confirm sheet decodes the simulated auth entry rather than printing free text.
Vocabulary: "Set up your wallet" (first time, including people who already own one, via "Have it already? Connect"), "Reconnect", "Your wallet is linked", "Account ID ...WXYZ", "Wallet: not set up" as a neutral chip; the wallet brand name only on the choose-a-wallet step, the install checklist and the "Open Freighter" button.

### Stake

What changes: "Stake from $5" opens the stake sheet for everyone. Dollar input with presets, "Minimum $5. Maximum $3,760 (what's left to reach the goal)", a summary with "Fee on stakes $0.00" (the phantom 3% is removed), a network-fee row that reads "Network fee: covered by BLKFNDR" once Phase 2 sponsorship exists and "about $0.01, paid in XLM from your wallet" in Phase 1 or whenever sponsorship is off, a plain preview of what happens to the money, then one gate card. The Phase 1 funds-short card is honest: practice dollars do not exist yet, so it points to XLM projects. The Confirm overlay previews the wallet popup, runs a countdown, and maps every contract code to a sentence. Success is an in-place receipt.
Custody stays honest: "You're moving $50 into the Riverside Clinic vault. It stays yours until the project meets its goal." The wallet, not the app, renders the real transaction in Phases 1 and 2.
Vocabulary: stake, "Review and confirm", "Leaves your wallet", "Receipt #123", "See the public record".

### Vote and get money back

What changes: notifications and email for vote opened, 24 hours left, approved, paid, ended short, refund ready, abandoned; a "Needs you / Updates" inbox; the Stages panel shows "Your say: $100 of the $1,501 needed", one "Approve this payout" button, and automatic execution of approved payouts and lapsed results by a platform cron (the same gas-only pattern as settle_stalled), with manual "Send it now" and "Close it now" kept under a Technical details disclosure for any signed-in stakeholder or the builder. Stakeholder status is read from the vault using the account's linked address, never from whether a wallet is connected right now. The refund sheet shows the exact amount from the contract's formula before confirming.
Custody stays honest: "Refunds are never sent automatically. Your wallet has to accept them, which is what stops anyone else from ever moving your money." The cron is labelled "BLKFNDR does this automatically; any stakeholder can also do it."
Vocabulary: stage, payout, "Approve this payout", "Vote ended short", "Collect your refund", "Returned $21.05", never Release, Settle, Claim or Window.

### Launch a project

What changes: a "Before you begin" checklist (identity verified, wallet set up, enough money for deposit and fee), a draft that autosaves and keys on the account, a currency choice written as "US dollars (USDC), recommended" vs "XLM, its value changes daily", a builder's deposit slider from the 5% minimum with the explanation filled in and no silent overwrite, a sticky cost card, a Funds check that checks deposit plus fee, a review that reports a storage-restore spike honestly for the phase it is in, and a success page instead of a redirect to an unindexed list.
Custody stays honest: the contract still pulls deposit and fee from the builder's own wallet in the transaction that creates the vault; the review says "Nothing is paid until you approve there."
Vocabulary: "Open the vault", "builder's deposit", "listing fee", stages, never Launch Campaign.

### Profile and identity

What changes: a "Getting set up" checklist, tabs "Your stakes · Your projects · Activity · Wallet", an "In your wallet" card that says where the money is, dollar amounts everywhere, activity as sentences, no red pill, stakeholders listed by name or "Stakeholder #12". Stakes key on the account's linked address; drafts and identity status key on the account. Identity verification opens to any signed-in user, attaches a wallet at submit time or later, accepts PDF, keeps buttons enabled with inline gaps, and its "See your verification record" opens an in-product panel.
Custody stays honest: "Only a verified mark is recorded against your wallet. Your documents and details are never published."
Vocabulary: "Verify your identity", "verified by a BLKFNDR reviewer", "See your verification record", "Account ID", "Receipt #123", "In your wallet".

## Open decisions for the owner

1. **Custody rule for the passkey wallet.** Decided (owner, 2026-10-02): non-custodial, no exceptions. The passkey wallet's only signer is the user's own device credential; BLKFNDR never holds a signer, a recovery key, or a reset power. Recovery is a second device or a user-held recovery key. It ships only when the wallet wasm is audited and hash-pinned with no platform-held admin or upgrade key, the client verifies on the ledger that the deployed wallet runs the pinned wasm hash and that its sole signer is the just-created passkey, and the Confirm sheet decodes in the browser every auth entry the passkey signs. Trade-off: no seed phrase and nothing to install, but a user who loses every device, their Apple or Google backup and any backup signer they added loses the money; BLKFNDR publishes the wallet code and the signing screen but holds no key that can upgrade, add a signer to, or act for a deployed wallet, and the README discloses the pinned wasm hash and that the only signer is the user's passkey.
2. **Which on-ramp.** Recommendation: contract a SEP-24 USDC anchor with MoneyGram Access, paying the user's own G-account first, and print "Pay by card" nowhere until it is live. Trade-off: anchors run their own identity check and vary by country, and most pay only classic accounts, so passkey wallets may need an anchor with contract-destination support, or, where no anchor pays a contract address, a G-account the user sets up and backs up themselves through the Prompt 9 wizard (a wallet they already hold, never a key the app generates in the browser), with copy that says the Face ID guarantees do not cover that account.
3. **Practice-mode messaging.** Recommendation: one persistent amber banner, a practice tag on every balance, the word testnet removed from the UI, and honest Phase 1 copy that practice dollars do not exist yet. Trade-off: it tells every visitor the product is not yet real, which is honest but will cost some sign-ups until mainnet.
4. **Freighter as the advanced path.** Recommendation: keep Freighter as one option inside the Stellar Wallets Kit chooser, default on desktop, named only on the choose-a-wallet step, the install checklist, the "Open Freighter" button and under Technical details, and keep it as the builder and owner path throughout. Trade-off: the brand still appears in a few places, but owners, admins and existing stakeholders keep working and an independently rendered transaction stays in every money path.
5. **The governance cron and the ops key.** Recommendation: from Phase 2 let the ops key, which holds XLM only and can pay network fees, pay the starting balance of new accounts (a plain payment, never a sponsored reserve) and submit permissionless calls but can never authorise a transfer or a vote, submit approved payouts, lapsed results and deadline closes, plus fee-bumps and account activations, labelled on screen as "BLKFNDR does this automatically; any stakeholder can also do it", with manual buttons under Technical details; release_milestone has no require_auth and is threshold-gated, so the key decides timing only. Decided 2026-10-02: allowed. The cron is a permissionless executor that can delay but never decide; it holds no key that can authorise a transfer or a vote, and the README names it as exactly that in Phase 2.
6. **Fee sponsorship budget.** Recommendation: fund fee-bumps from the Operations Vault with per-account daily caps, a contract allowlist and a kill switch, and keep the "about $0.01 in XLM" self-pay fallback in every sheet. Trade-off: griefing can drain the budget, and an outage inside a 7-day vote window sends users to the fallback row, which is the old experience.
7. **Auto-claim refunds (decided 2026-10-02: not allowed).** No policy signer, co-signer or platform key may ever call claim_refund for a user. Refunds are collected only by the stakeholder's own wallet; notifications and the one-tap refund sheet are the whole mechanism. Trade-off: unclaimed money stays in vaults, but the money path never contains a platform-initiated call.
